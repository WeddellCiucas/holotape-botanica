*** APPENDIX B-1 ***
INTERVIEW: MOIRA BROWN

SUBJECT: MOIRA BROWN (Shopkeeper / 'Book Author?')
LOCATION: Craterside Supply, Megaton
INTERVIEWER: Dr. Aloïs Klein

──────────────────────────── 


2:00:04 [A. KLEIN] Ja, gut. Miss Brown. The recorder is running, we may begin.

*A sound of a pen being clicked can be heard.*

2:00:14 [M. BROWN] Oh, this is so exciting! I've never had my guide be peer-reviewed before! This is so official.

2:00:22 [A. KLEIN] Ach danke, Miss Brown. But please, mind the microphone cable. We are aiming for a structured analysis here, so if we could refrain from... spontaneous field demonstrations until we cover the basic botanical entries?

*Moira clears her throat.*

2:00:36 [M. BROWN] Ah, right, of course, Director. I'm sorry. No irradiated samples, promise! Really, everyone is used to radiation here, I'm surprised to hear you guys aren't up there. It's almost cute.

2:00:48 [A. KLEIN] 'Niedlich'? 'Cute' is absolutely not the terminology I would apply to cellular degeneration and radiation sickness, Miss Brown.

2:00:58 [M. BROWN] Oh, it just all depends on how sturdy you are! A few years back, I had this wonderful field research buddy who helped me study radiation sickness up close. Never did catch his real name but everyone around here just called him the 'Lone Wanderer.' For a Vault Dweller, boy, he proved to be mighty resilient!

*A beat of silence followed by the squeak of leather as Aloïs shifts in his chair.*

2:01:21 [A. KLEIN] Um Himmels Willen. You subjected a civilian to intentional high-dose radiation exposure for an informal field guide? 

2:01:31 [M. BROWN] Well, he volunteered! And he didn't grow another head or weird rashes, so really, win-win!

*Deep breaths. Sounds of paper rustling, and pen scribbling over paper.*

2:01:45 [A. KLEIN] Mein Gott, it is entirely my own fault for asking.

2:01:54 [M. BROWN] Ooh, is that a red pen? Is that what makes it official?

2:02:08 [A. KLEIN] It is not a cosmetic choice, Miss Brown. The red ink signifies critical errors. And given the absolute lack of control groups, standardized dosages, or basic laboratory safety in this document, my pen will likely run dry before we reach page three.

2:02:24 [M. BROWN] Oh, perfect! That just means after you're through with it, my guide is gonna be so much better! There will be proper science backing it.

2:02:37 [A. KLEIN] Your optimism is alarming, Miss. Highly alarming.

*Aloïs clears his throat.*

2:02:45 [A. KLEIN] So. Your botanical entries. I see here you have written an entry about a ... mutfruit plant. Care to elaborate?

2:02:59 [M. BROWN] Oh, Mutfruits are wonderful! They’re big, purple, crunchy, and they grow just about everywhere there’s a pile of dirt and a little bit of bad water. Super sweet, too, if you don't mind the extra crunch from the little glowing bits inside!

The rapid, sharp scratching of a fountain pen against paper fills the silence.

2:03:15 [A. KLEIN] 'Glowing bits.' Wunderbar. So, rather than analyzing the mutational bio-accumulation of radioisotopes in the pericarp, you simply ate it and decided it was sweet?

2:03:28 [M. BROWN] Well, yeah! You can't tell if a fruit is good for a trek just by staring at it with a magnifying glass, Director! You've gotta bite into it! It's a staple wasteland crop. But you see, travelers from Rivet City have traded with me these refilled stimpak syringes. 

*Pause.*

2:03:38 [A. KLEIN] Refilled?

2:03:39 [M. BROWN] Refilled, yes! Apparently, their hydroponic scientists have discovered that purifying a mutfruit, cleaning out those radioiso particles, can help them create a healing agent that works a lot like a stimpak. 

*The scratching of Aloïs's pen stops.*

2:03:52 [A. KLEIN] Warten Sie. You are telling me that an isolated wasteland settlement managed to isolate the active alkaloid compounds from an irradiated pericarp, purge the radioisotopes, and synthesize a viable biological regenerative agent? 

2:04:10 [M. BROWN] Yes! Doesn't pack quite the same immediate punch as a shiny Pre-War Med-Tek original, but if you don't have anything better on hand, it does the trick real nice! Maybe someone can improve it. 

2:04:26 [A. KLEIN] Erstaunlich... Actual scientific methodology in the Capital Wasteland. I must review their purification protocols immediately.

2:04:38 [M. BROWN] Not until the peer-review is done, Director. But Rivet City is that way. You know, now that I think about it, you remind me of somebody. 

2:04:46 [A. KLEIN] I beg your pardon? 

2:04:48 [M. BROWN] Your eyes are very familiar. And your accent. There was this girl running around. Blue eyes, this short, metal arm... 

2:05:02 [A. KLEIN] Warten Sie. Blue eyes? A metallic prosthetic on the left arm?

*Sudden, sharp scrape of a chair against the floorboards as Aloïs stands up.*

2:05:12 [A. KLEIN] Miss Brown, please! Tell me you are talking about Lily! Is she safe? Where did you see her?

2:05:25 [M. BROWN] Woah, easy there, Director! Don't push your chair over, it took me three weeks to fix that leg! Oh, yes, Lily! That was her name! She came tumbling through Megaton's gates about a month ago. Bright pink hair, giant smile, and well, a bit banged up, but completely fine!

2:05:43 [A. KLEIN] Vor einem Monat?! A whole month?! Ach, Gott... What kind of trouble did that idiot girl get herself into across the ocean? Did she tell you where she was heading next?

2:05:56 [M. BROWN] Well, she bought all the Nuka-Cola I had in stock, a few bobbypins and the one high-grade lubricant I had left, and said she was heading for the swamp? To the Pitt? To Rivet City? I'm not entirely sure. Actually, Rivet City might know where she's off to. Or you can ask a Brotherhood patrol. 

2:06:12 [A. KLEIN] 'The swamp, the Pitt, or Rivet City.' That is a range of roughly three hundred kilometers in entirely opposite directions, Miss Brown. That tells me absolutely nothing!

*The sound of heavy, rapid pacing against the metal flooring.*

2:06:26 [A. KLEIN] Ach, natürlich. Of course she needed lubricant for a prosthetic she undoubtedly acquired doing something reckless!

2:06:37 [M. BROWN] Hey, on the bright side, she was in super high spirits! She even left me a little drawing of a plant she found out in the ruins—said her brother would 'nerd out' over it if he ever caught up. Want me to pull it up from the back?

*Aloïs stops pacing instantly.*

2:06:51 [A. KLEIN] Ja. Yes. Please. Show me the drawing.

2:07:05 [M. BROWN] Here it is! She drew it right on the back of a Nuka-Cola Quantum flyer. See? Big round leaves, little spiky stem... she called it a 'Spark-Lily' or something like that.

*Sounds of paper rustling as Aloïs takes the flyer, followed by a soft inhale.*

2:07:22 [A. KLEIN] Das ist Electric Delphinium. That species only germinates in heavily irradiated, low-pH wetlands. Which means...

2:07:34 [M. BROWN] Point Lookout! Oh, see? I told you it’d make sense to a smart guy like you! She definitely took the riverboat down south to the swamps!

*Aloïs clicks his pen.*

2:07:44 [A. KLEIN] Point Lookout. Gott steh mir bei... She went into a coastal radiation marsh by herself.

2:07:54 [M. BROWN] So, are we gonna finish the peer-review first, or are you rushing off to the docks?

2:08:02 [A. KLEIN] We are finishing this review, Miss Brown. A promise is a promise. Page three. Proceed.
